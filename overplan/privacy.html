Privacy by architecture

Privacy Policy

Overplan for iPhone, iPad and Mac · Last updated 17 August 2026

Your rooms stay yours.

Overplan collects no personal data. There is no account, no mcfinapps server, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. The app makes no network requests of its own and works with the device offline, start to finish.

This policy covers Overplan on iPhone, iPad and Mac. It is one app, one document format and one set of promises, so almost everything below is true on all three. Where a platform differs — a Mac has no LiDAR sensor, and it keeps its files somewhere else — the paragraph says so with a badge like this oneMacand a paragraph with no badge holds everywhere.

What Overplan can reach

Camera and LiDARiPhone · iPad

Scanning a room uses your device's camera and LiDAR sensor through Apple's ARKit and RoomPlan frameworks, which measure the walls as you walk around. Camera access is the only permission Overplan ever asks for, and it is asked for at the moment you first start a scan, not at launch.

  • The camera view and the depth stream are processed on your device and are never stored or uploaded. What RoomPlan hands back is geometry — walls, floors, doors, windows and their dimensions — and that geometry is what Overplan saves.
  • The camera runs only while the scanner is on screen. Leaving the scanner ends the session.
  • No photographs or video are recorded. Overplan has no way to take a picture, and nothing in the app writes an image of what the camera saw.

Everything else in the app — sketching, tracing, editing, measuring, viewing in AR, PDF and 3D export — needs no camera at all, and works on a device with no LiDAR sensor.

MacThe Mac app asks for no permissions whatsoever, and cannot ask. A Mac has no LiDAR sensor, so there is no scanner in it: ARKit, RoomPlan and the scanning code are not built into that app at all. It declares no camera purpose and holds no camera entitlement, which is what makes this a fact about the binary rather than a promise about our intentions — the system would refuse it a camera even if a future bug asked. Scan on an iPhone or iPad, then open the plan on the Mac to do the editing and exporting.

Files you pick, and nothing else

Overplan can open three kinds of file, and each one arrives the same way: you pick it in the system document picker, and the app is given that one file. It cannot browse your storage, list your documents, or open anything you did not choose.

  • An image or PDF to trace over — a photo of a paper plan, a floor plan from an agent, a scan of a sketch. PNG, JPEG, HEIC or the first page of a PDF.
  • A 3D model to place in a room — USDZ, USD, GLB, glTF or OBJ.
  • An .overplan plan sent from another device, downloaded, or sitting in Files or in a folder of your own.

The photo library is not involved. Even "a photo of a paper plan" is chosen through the document picker, not through Photos — so Overplan never requests photo library access and has no view of your photos, their contents or their location data.

A file you pick is copied into Overplan's own storage so the plan keeps working after the original moves or is deleted. Your original is not modified.

MacThe Mac app runs in Apple's App Sandbox, which is the same arrangement described in stronger terms: the only files it can read or write are the ones you name in an Open or Save panel, or drag onto its window, and it holds no entitlement for your Documents folder, your Desktop, your Downloads or anything else. It also cannot browse or list your disk. Choosing a file is the grant, and the grant is that one file.

One thing the Mac app does store about your files, and it is worth being exact about: when you open a plan it saves a small system-issued token for that file — a security-scoped bookmark — so it can reopen the same document after you quit and relaunch, and so Open Recent leads somewhere rather than to a permission error. A bookmark identifies where one file you chose lives. It is kept in the app's own container on your Mac, it is the mechanism the sandbox provides for exactly this purpose, and nothing transmits it anywhere.

What it never asks for

iPhone · iPadOverplan declares exactly one permission purpose in its app bundle — the camera one above. There is no code path, and no declared purpose, that would let it request or receive:

  • your photo library, microphone, location, contacts, calendar, reminders, health data, motion & fitness activity, Bluetooth, local network, or notifications;
  • your Apple Account, or any account of any kind — Overplan has no sign-in;
  • an advertising identifier. Overplan does not ask for tracking permission because it has nothing to track you with.

If iOS ever shows you a permission prompt from Overplan that is not the camera one, that is a bug worth reporting, not a feature.

MacThe Mac version's equivalent list is its sandbox entitlements, and it is short enough to print in full. It holds exactly four, and you can check them yourself on the app in your Applications folder:

  • The sandbox itself — the thing that enforces everything else here.
  • Read and write the files you select — an Open or Save panel's own grant.
  • App-scope bookmarks — the tokens described above, so a document you opened yesterday still opens today.
  • Outgoing network connections — which sounds like the opposite of every promise on this page, and is not. It is explained in The app makes no network requests below, because it deserves a straight answer rather than a footnote.

There is no camera, microphone, location, address book, calendar, printing, Apple Events, USB, Bluetooth, incoming-network or arbitrary-file entitlement — and no entitlement for your Documents, Desktop, Downloads or Pictures folders.

Where your plans live

Your plans are ordinary files, and they are yours

iPhone · iPadEvery plan is one .overplan file in Overplan's Documents folder on your device, and that folder is deliberately visible to you: open the Files app and look under On My iPhone (or On My iPad) → Overplan.

From there you can copy, move, rename, organise, AirDrop, back up or delete your plans like any other document — including copying one into iCloud Drive or onto a computer. That is your decision to make, and Overplan never makes it for you: the app has no iCloud container, does not sync, and puts nothing anywhere except its own folder on this device.

The flip side of a visible folder is worth saying plainly: anything with access to your device's files — another app you grant access to, a computer you connect to, a backup service you have set up — can reach your plans there, exactly as it can reach any other document. mcfinapps cannot.

MacThe Mac keeps plans in two kinds of place, and you choose which. A plan you open by double-clicking it, or through File ▸ Open Plan…, stays exactly where you keep it — your own folder, an external disk, iCloud Drive — and is edited there. Nothing is copied. The app's own library holds its copies inside its sandbox container, at ~/Library/Containers/com.mcmike.overplan.mac/Data/Library/Application Support/Overplan/Plans.

MacThat is a less convenient location than iOS's, and it is a deliberate choice rather than an oversight: your Documents folder is yours, and an app that plants a folder in it uninvited is not the kind of app this is. To have a plan somewhere you picked, use Export and choose Project package (.overplan) — which is the same one-file-per-plan document iOS keeps, openable on any of the three platforms.

What a plan contains

An .overplan file carries the room itself: walls, openings, ceiling height, room names and areas, the furniture you have placed, your measurements and their captions, and the original scan or drawing the plan started from, kept separately so it can be restored.

It describes the layout and dimensions of a real place, which for most people is a home. Treat sharing one the way you would treat sharing a floor plan of where you live.

Imported images and models

A photo or PDF you trace over is stored with the plan that used it, so the plan can show you later what it was traced from. A 3D model you import is copied once into an ImportedModels folder in the app's own storage — Overplan → ImportedModels in Files on an iPhone or iPad — and shared by every plan that places it.

iPhone · iPadBoth are in Files alongside your plans, for the same reason your plans are: you gave the app those files, so you are entitled to see them, back them up and take them away again. Deleting them there is enough; nothing keeps a second copy elsewhere.

MacThe same two copies are made, in the same shape, inside the app's sandbox container rather than in a folder of yours. Your originals are read once and never written to, and removing the app removes its container and everything in it.

Settings, and the sample room

Your unit choice, haptics switch and every Advanced dial are stored in the app's own preferences on this device — inside its sandbox container on a Mac. They are settings, not personal data, they are not shared between your devices, and they go nowhere.

The sample room Overplan copies into your library on first launch ships inside the app. It is a made-up room, not anyone's home, and it becomes an ordinary plan of yours the moment it is copied — rename it, edit it or delete it freely.

Device backups

Because your plans are in the app's own storage, your operating system may include them in a device backup — an iPhone or iPad backup, or a Mac's Time Machine — according to the backup settings you control. Those backups are provided and governed by Apple, not by mcfinapps, and we have no access to them.

What leaves the device

Sharing, which is always your action

Overplan can hand out seven things, all generated on your device, all from the Share screen:

  • The plan itself — the editable .overplan project.
  • Three PDFs — a to-scale Drawing with dimensions, a True scale (1:1) drawing for CAD, and a rendered Presentation view.
  • Three 3D models — USDZ, GLB and OBJ (with its .mtl companion).

Every one of them can reveal the layout, dimensions and openings of the space you scanned. None of them is created or sent automatically. A file leaves Overplan only when you choose it, and then choose a person, app, device or storage service in Apple's standard share sheet.

MacThe Mac offers the same seven, in two commands rather than one: Share hands the file to the system share sheet, and Export writes it to a location you name in a Save panel. Both are your action, both produce the file on your own machine, and the Save panel is also what grants the app permission to write there.

Once you have shared a file, that copy is out of Overplan's hands and out of ours. The recipient, and any service you sent it through, handles it under their own terms.

Viewing your room in ARiPhone · iPad

View in AR builds a 3D model of your room on your device and opens it in Apple's Quick Look viewer, which uses the camera to place it in the space around you. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is recorded, and no file is handed to anyone — the model is prepared on your device and shown to you. It becomes a shared file only if you then choose to share it.

Quick Look is Apple's, and while it is open the camera is in its hands rather than Overplan's. It uses the live view to find a surface and draws your model onto it; nothing about that view is recorded, and Overplan is handed nothing back — no photograph, no video, no measurement of the room you are standing in.

MacThere is no AR on the Mac and no button for it: no pass-through camera, so nothing to place a model in. It is not a disabled feature — the AR code is not in that app at all.

The app makes no network requests

This is a statement about the code, not an intention. Overplan contains no networking of any kind: no server calls, no cloud storage, no sync, no remote configuration, no third-party libraries of any sort, and no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting service. Everything it draws, renders, bakes and exports it does with what is already on the device.

One channel is worth naming because it is not ours: Overplan never sends a crash report, but Apple's own system can. If you are testing a beta through TestFlight, Apple collects crashes and passes them on as part of how TestFlight works. On a copy from the App Store it does the same only if you have switched on Apple's own analytics sharing in Settings. Those reports come from Apple rather than from this app, they describe where the code stopped, and they contain no part of your plans.

The Help and Privacy pages you are reading in the app are bundled copies rendered from local files, so even they fetch nothing. Put the device in Airplane Mode and every part of Overplan still works.

MacAnd now the awkward part, stated plainly rather than left for you to find: the Mac app declares the "outgoing network connections" entitlement. Everything above is still true — it makes no network request of any kind — and this is why the entitlement is nevertheless there.

MacThese two pages are shown inside the app by the system's own web view, loading the HTML file bundled in the app. A sandboxed Mac app cannot start that web view's helper process at all without the network entitlement — even for a local file with nowhere to go. Without it the Help window opens, draws its title bar, and shows an empty white rectangle for ever, with no error anywhere. That is not a theory; it is the bug that put the entitlement in the app, and it is a documented WebKit requirement rather than anything Overplan wanted.

MacSo the entitlement grants a capability the app never uses. If you would rather verify that than take our word for it, the app is on your own machine: a network monitor, or Little Snitch, or macOS's own firewall will show you Overplan making no connections, because there is no server to connect to and no code that would connect to one. If that ever changes, this page changes first — see Changes to this policy.

Two taps hand off to another app, and they are the only two: the email address below opens Mail, and the mcfinapps credit link at the end of the Help page opens your browser. Both need a deliberate tap or click, both are visible before you use them, and neither sends anything about you. Handing a link to the system is not the app connecting to anything.

What we collect

Nothing

Overplan has no account system, no cloud database, no analytics service, no advertising SDK, no tracking SDK and no third-party crash reporting. Because mcfinapps receives no data from the app at all, there is no personal data held by us to access, correct, export, sell or delete — and no request you could make to us would produce any, because none exists.

We also cannot identify you, count you, or tell whether you use the app. That is the intended outcome, and it is why there is no usage-based feature in Overplan of any kind.

What the App Store shows

Overplan's privacy manifest declares no tracking, no tracking domains and no collected data types. The one required-reason API it declares is the system preferences store, used only to remember your own settings inside the app. Apple requires that manifest of the iPhone and iPad app; the Mac app collects and tracks exactly as much, which is nothing, and the App Store's privacy label is the same label for all three.

Apple's own App Store record — purchases, downloads and receipts — is between you and Apple, under Apple's privacy policy. Overplan is a pay-once app with no subscriptions, no in-app purchases and no advertising, so there is nothing further to report there either.

Apple's own diagnostics

If you have separately enabled Apple's option to share diagnostics and usage data with app developers, Apple may provide us with limited crash and performance information under Apple's own privacy terms. That comes from the operating system, not from Overplan — the app transmits nothing itself — and it is a setting you control, under Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements in Settings on an iPhone or iPad, and in System Settings on a Mac.

What this policy cannot promise

The honest limits

An app that keeps everything on your device also cannot do some things for you, and it is better to say so than to imply otherwise:

  • We cannot recover a plan you delete. There is no copy on a server, because there is no server. Your backups are the only way back.
  • We cannot unshare a file. Once a PDF, a 3D model or an .overplan has left your device, retrieving it is between you and whoever received it.
  • We cannot protect a plan from your device. Overplan's storage is protected by the operating system's own file protection — your passcode on an iPhone or iPad, your account and FileVault on a Mac — like any app's; it is not separately encrypted by us, and it does not have its own password.
  • We cannot see anything to help you with. A support conversation depends entirely on what you tell us, because we have nothing else.

Children, changes and contact

Children

Overplan does not knowingly collect information from children — or from anyone else. It requires no account, gathers no personal information, and contains no advertising, no social features and no links to user-generated content.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the revised version is posted here with a new date, and the copy inside the app is updated with the next release. Any future feature that would materially change how data is handled will be described here before it is used — and if Overplan ever gained a feature that needed the network, this page would say so plainly rather than quietly dropping the claim above.
