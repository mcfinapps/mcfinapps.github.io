Real Overplan interface · no generated UI

01 · One app

Same plan.
Every screen you own.

Not a phone app with a companion. One app, built once, native on each: the pocket where you scan, the tablet where you draw, the desk where you finish. Same tools, same file, same clay. Buy it once.

02 · Create

Scan it.
Or draw it.

Point an iPhone or iPad with LiDAR at the room and walk it — Overplan keeps the walls and throws away the mess. No LiDAR, or a room that no longer exists? Draw it instead, on a snapping grid, with a photo or PDF of the old floor plan underneath to trace.

03 · The look

The room as space.
Not as stuff.

Everything is one material — a warm, unglazed clay, lit from a single soft source, with ambient occlusion baked in where surfaces meet. No photo textures, no fake sunlight, no visual noise competing with the thing you are trying to read. It looks like a model an architect would put on a table, because that is what it is for.

One material. One light. Nothing shouting.

04 · Edit

Shape it until it’s right.

A scan is a starting point, never an answer. Drag a wall and the rooms follow. Slide a window and two dimension lines tell you the gap either side, so centring it needs no arithmetic. Tap a number to type an exact one.

05 · Share

Four ways out.

The plan is not the point. What you can hand someone is the point — a builder, a client, a landlord, a 3D artist, or your partner who cannot picture it.

06 · The one to show people

Put the room
on your table.

Overplan writes a USDZ, and every iPhone and iPad made in the last several years already knows how to open one. Tap the link, point the camera at a desk, and the room is sitting there — walls, doors, furniture, shadows, at whatever size you like.

Nobody has to install Overplan to see it. That is the whole trick.

Tap the card on iPhone or iPad to drop this real Overplan room into the room you are in. On a Mac it opens in Quick Look. 2.7 MB · no app required
